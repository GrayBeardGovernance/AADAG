# AADAG Principles

## 1. Govern decisions, not merely tools

The same AI capability can create very different risks depending on the decision it influences.

## 2. Accountability cannot be automated away

An organization remains responsible for decisions made in its name, even when AI contributes to or executes them.

## 3. Human involvement must be meaningful

A nominal approval click is not meaningful oversight. A reviewer needs adequate authority, time, information, and ability to disagree.

## 4. Safeguards should follow consequences

Low-consequence assistance should not carry the same burden as decisions affecting rights, safety, livelihood, access, or opportunity.

## 5. Influence matters as much as capability

Governance must examine the weight given to an AI output, not only what the system is technically capable of producing.

## 6. Decisions should be explainable enough to examine

Affected people and responsible officials need sufficient traceability to understand, review, and challenge consequential outcomes.

## 7. Recourse is part of governance

A decision process is incomplete if incorrect outcomes cannot be questioned, corrected, or reversed.

## 8. Context changes risk

A safe use in one setting may be unacceptable in another. Decision consequence, population, environment, and operational dependence all matter.

## 9. Governance must remain usable

Controls that cannot be understood or applied will become paperwork. AADAG should be rigorous enough to matter and simple enough to use.

## 10. The framework itself must be challengeable

AADAG will improve through documented criticism, examples, testing, and revision.
