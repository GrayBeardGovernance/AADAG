# AADAG Principles

## 1. Govern every decision AI helps shape

The same AI capability can create very different risks depending on the decision it influences.

## 2. Accountability stays with the organization

An organization remains responsible for decisions made in its name, including decisions assisted or executed by AI.

## 3. Human involvement must be meaningful

A human reviewer needs adequate authority, time, information, and freedom to disagree.

## 4. Safeguards should follow consequences

Decisions affecting rights, safety, livelihood, access, or opportunity deserve stronger safeguards than low-consequence assistance.

## 5. Influence matters as much as capability

Governance must examine both what an AI system can produce and how much weight its output carries in the decision.

## 6. Decisions should be explainable enough to examine

Affected people and responsible officials need sufficient traceability to understand, review, and challenge consequential outcomes.

## 7. Recourse is part of governance

A complete decision process gives incorrect outcomes a clear path for challenge, correction, and reversal.

## 8. Context changes risk

Decision consequence, population, environment, and operational dependence determine whether an AI use is appropriate in a particular setting.

## 9. Governance must remain usable

Effective controls combine rigor with language and processes that people can understand and apply.

## 10. The framework itself must be challengeable

AADAG will improve through documented criticism, examples, testing, and revision.
